Why Most Freelancers Get Their Rates Wrong

  • They price by emotion — "I'm new, so I'll charge ₹100" is a panic move, not a business decision.
  • They copy other countries' rates — a US rate isn't an Indian rate, and undercutting yourself only attracts low-quality clients.
  • They forget to value experience — the same skill at 2 years vs 7 years of experience is worth 2–3x more.
  • They ignore scope — charging per project without estimating hours means you're working for free.

Rate Calculator for Clients: How Much Should a Project Cost?

Hiring side is just as tricky. If your budget is too low, you only attract inexperienced freelancers. If it's too high, you're overpaying. The budget calculator uses the same market data to tell you what a project should realistically cost — so you can post a budget that attracts serious talent without wasting money.

Quick rule of thumb: take the hourly rate for the skill, multiply by the number of hours you think the project needs, then add a buffer for revisions. The calculator does this for you automatically.

When Should You Raise Your Rates?

Use the calculator as a baseline, not a ceiling. You should raise your rates when:

  • You've completed 10+ projects with 5-star reviews
  • You've specialised in a niche (specialists earn 2–3x generalists)
  • You deliver consistently early with zero supervision
  • Your current rate has attracted more clients than you can handle
