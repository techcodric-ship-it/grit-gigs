The 50% Advance Trap: How Fake Freelancers Scam Indian Businesses

September 16, 2026 · 8 min read · By Amuthavanan
"Sir, kaam acha hoga, 50% advance bhej do, aaj hi start karta hoon." — and ₹40,000 left my account. The WhatsApp number was switched off by evening. I never got the logo. I never got my money back.

That's a real message from a boutique owner in Indore. And before you think "that won't happen to me" — it happens to thousands of Indian business owners every single month. Fake freelancers on WhatsApp, Instagram, and Facebook are running the oldest, most effective scam in India: take the advance, then vanish.

Here's the uncomfortable part: it works because most freelancers genuinely DO ask for advances — so you can't tell real ones from fake ones. By the time you learn the difference, your money is already gone.

This guide is the opposite of a horror story. It's the 6-rule system that makes it impossible to lose a rupee to a fake freelancer — and it works whether you hire through Grit&Gigs or anywhere else.

Rule 1: The Advance Is Not the Problem — The Untracked Advance Is

Reframe this right now, because it's the difference between being safe and being "lucky":

Direct advance to a stranger
Money leaves your UPI. It's gone. There's no record that means anything, no one to hold accountable, no system. You're betting on a stranger's honesty with real money.
Escrow-held payment
Money sits in a secure account. The freelancer only gets it after you approve the work. If they vanish, you raise a dispute and the money is protected. The freelancer has no reason to run — they literally can't reach the money.

Same freelancer, same project, same money. In one system you're gambling; in the other honesty is the only option. That's why escrow payments are the single biggest protection an Indian client can use — and why Grit&Gigs built every payment on Razorpay escrow.

Rule 2: Know the 8 Red Flags of a Fake Freelancer

Forward this list to every business owner you know. This is what a scam looks like in India right now:

  • 1They ask for 50–100% advance before showing a single deliverable. Real freelancers work in milestones or accept escrow — they don't need your full trust upfront.
  • 2The price is suspiciously low. ₹800 for a full website, ₹300 for a logo. Scammers don't price to be paid fairly — they price to hook you fast.
  • 3Refuses a video call. "Meri video cam nahi hai" is a red flag. A real professional will happily talk once. A scammer stays invisible.
  • 4Portfolio is screenshots of other people's work. Reverse-image-search their samples (Google Images) — stolen portfolios are the most common tell.
  • 5"My gateway is down, UPI directly." The instant your payment stops being trackable, you've lost your leverage.
  • 6Pressure and urgency. "Only 1 slot left today," "offer ends tonight." Scammers rush you so you don't think.
  • 7Phone never verified / matches nothing. A name that doesn't match the UPI ID, a number with no WhatsApp photo, a "company" with no address.
  • 8Files that are "locked," endless "1 more day," and then silence. By the time you realize, the number is dead.

One red flag alone isn't proof. Two or three together? Walk away. No deal is worth betting real money on a red flag parade.

Rule 3: Check the 5 Green Flags Before You Hire

  • ✓Identity verified. On Grit&Gigs, freelancers are KYC-verified with government ID — you know exactly whose UPI you're paying, because the platform recorded it.
  • ✓A portfolio you can verify — live links, past client names, case studies. Not just pretty screenshots.
  • ✓Reviews from paid, completed projects. Reviews that required real money to change hands are nearly impossible to fake.
  • ✓Willingness to work in milestones or escrow. A real professional is happy to be paid on delivery. That's the test that exposes 90% of fakes.
  • ✓Fast, clear communication. Real freelancers answer properly. Scammers use copy-paste sweet talk — then disappear when you ask real questions.

Rule 5: Never Pay Directly. Ever.

This is the whole system in one sentence: money you can't recover shouldn't leave your account.

Payments should only happen in three acceptable ways:

  1. Escrow on a platform — money held safely, released to the freelancer only after you approve the work (this is exactly what Grit&Gigs does via Razorpay).
  2. Milestone-based — big projects split into phases; you release payment for each phase only when the work is right.
  3. Payment on delivery — for tiny, low-risk jobs, pay only when the file is in your hand.

Anything else — advance, deposit, "refundable seat," "package unlock" — is a transfer that cannot be reversed once it moves. UPI is instant for good reasons. It's also instant for scammers.

Rule 6: If Something Goes Wrong, Do These 4 Things

  1. Report on cybercrime.gov.in or call 1930 immediately — the earlier you report, the higher the chance of tracing the transaction.
  2. Report the UPI transaction to your bank and to UPI (UPI Chargeback). Attach chat + payment screenshots.
  3. Report the number on WhatsApp (Report > Spam/Block). If it's an Instagram account, report it too.
  4. Tell other business owners. Scammers reuse the same number and script for months. One forwarded message can save the next victim.

But the honest truth? Prevention beats recovery. Recovering money from a direct UPI scam is rare. Holding it in escrow means it never disappears in the first place.
