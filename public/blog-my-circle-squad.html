Grit Circle & Squad Plan — How to Form a 6-Person Freelance Team and Win Bigger Projects

Published September 5, 2026 • 6 min read

Solo work builds income. Squad work builds empires.

That's the idea behind Grit Circles (squads) on Grit&Gigs — a way for freelancers who already trade skills, earn, and review each other to come together and bid on projects too big for one person.

Here's what a Grit Circle actually is, how it works, and whether you should start one.

The Ceiling of Working Solo

As a solo freelancer, every project is a trade-off:

  • You can only take projects within your single skill set
  • A site build needs design + code + content — you can do one, maybe two
  • Your monthly income is capped by the hours in a day
  • Big clients hire teams, not individuals

A client who wants a full product — design, backend, frontend, SEO, launch content — doesn't want to coordinate five freelancers separately. They want one team, one quote, one deadline.

That's exactly the gap Grit Circles fill.

What Is a Grit Circle?

A Grit Circle is a team of up to 6 freelancers (Squad plan) led by a leader. Circles list squad services and bid on projects together. When a squad order or project is completed, the payout is split equally across the members, and each member is charged their own plan commission.

Members can join a circle by invitation or by join request — the leader approves.

Two terms, one idea: "My Circle" is the team you see in your dashboard. "Squad" is the paid plan that unlocks it. When you hear "squad", think "a circle on the Squad plan".

How a Grit Circle Works — 3 Steps

1. Form your circle

Start a Grit Circle and invite 4–6 freelancers you trust — a designer, a developer, a writer, a marketer. Each member brings the skill the next project needs. You're the leader: only you can edit settings, approve members, and close the circle.

2. Bid as a team

Squads publish squad services and win multi-skill projects solo freelancers can't deliver — the whole circle works, the whole circle earns. Your circle's bid carries the combined reputation and ratings of every member.

3. Deliver & split fairly

Agree your payout split, deliver together, and build a shared reputation that compounds on every win. Payouts are handled transparently on the platform — no chasing each other for money after a project.

The 4-Member Rule for Squad Projects

Some clients post projects specifically as SQUAD projects — jobs they want a team to deliver. To bid on those, your circle needs to be real:

Squad projects can only be bid on by a Grit Circle with at least 4 active members. Grow your circle to unlock squad project bids.

That rule keeps squad rounds honest — a "squad" under 4 members is usually one freelancer wearing five hats, which is exactly what clients on the platform are trying to avoid.

How Payouts Get Split

When a squad order or project is completed and the client approves, the payout is split equally across the circle members who delivered, and each member is charged their own plan commission.

Example — a ₹1,20,000 project won by a 4-member circle on the Squad plan:

Line item Amount
Project payout ₹1,20,000
Split equally across 4 members ₹30,000 each
Squad plan commission (1%) ₹1,200 total
Each member keeps (before their own plan commission) ₹29,700

No arguments over who did "more". No one chasing anyone for their share. The platform splits it, and the review that follows lands on your circle's shared reputation.

A Real Circle in Action

Imagine a circle called Pixel Forge — a founder plus a UI/UX designer, a backend engineer, and a content & SEO writer. Five members on the Squad plan.

A client posts a SQUAD project: "Build our SaaS landing page + billing flow + launch content" with a budget of ₹1,20,000 and a 14-day deadline.

Pixel Forge bids as one team. The designer owns the interface, the engineer builds the billing flow, the writer produces the launch content. The client deals with one circle, one quote, one delivery date.

One person couldn't have delivered that brief alone. A 5-member circle does it in 14 days, splits the payout, and adds a 5-star review to its circle profile — the same way solo ratings compound, but faster, because the wins are bigger.
