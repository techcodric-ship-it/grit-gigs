I Quit My ₹8 LPA Job to Freelance — Here's What Happened in 2026

August 23, 2026 · 8 min read · By Amuthavanan

March 15, 2026. I walked out of my cubicle at 6:47 PM, opened my laptop one last time, and typed my resignation email. Two years of a "stable" ₹8 LPA software job. Good salary for a fresher. Proud parents. Relatives who finally stopped asking "when will you get a real job?"

I quit.

My manager called me the next morning. "Are you sure?" Yes. "Do you have another offer?" No. "Then what's the plan?" Freelancing.

He laughed. My parents panicked. My friends thought I'd lost my mind.

Here's what actually happened.

Why I Quit (It Wasn't What You Think)

It wasn't rebellion. It wasn't a "passion project." It was math.

At ₹8 LPA, my take-home was about ₹52,000/month. After rent (₹15,000), food (₹8,000), transport (₹4,000), EMI (₹6,000), and "life" (₹5,000), I was saving roughly ₹14,000/month. At that rate, I'd need 18 years to save ₹30 lakh for a house down payment.

Meanwhile, I saw freelancers on Grit&Gigs and other platforms charging ₹2,000-5,000 per website, ₹1,500 per brand identity, ₹800 per blog post. Some were earning ₹1-2 lakh/month working fewer hours than my 9-to-6 (which was really 9-to-8).

The math wasn't mathing for my job.

Month 1: The Terrifying Zero

I won't sugarcoat it. The first month was brutal.

No clients. No income. No structure. I woke up at 10 AM, stared at my laptop, applied to 15 freelance gigs, got 2 replies, and both said "we went with someone cheaper."

My savings were ₹87,000. My rent was ₹15,000. I had maybe 4 months before I'd have to crawl back to a job.

Here's what I did differently in Week 3:

The Shift That Changed Everything

I stopped waiting for clients to find me. Instead, I listed my skills on Grit&Gigs — web development, landing pages, and React apps. But here's the key: I also listed what I needed. I needed a logo, social media posts, and someone to write my portfolio copy.

Within 48 hours, I got matched with a graphic designer who needed a website for her photography business. No money exchanged hands — we bartered. I built her site. She designed my brand identity. Both of us got what we needed without spending a rupee.

That one barter gave me a portfolio piece, a testimonial, and the confidence that this could actually work.

Month 2: First Real Client (₹12,000)

My barter partner referred me to a small business owner in Chennai who needed an e-commerce website. He'd been quoted ₹45,000 by an agency. I offered to do it for ₹12,000 — my first paid gig.

I delivered it in 9 days. He was shocked. The agency had told him 6 weeks.

He paid me through Grit&Gigs escrow. The money hit my wallet in 24 hours. ₹12,000. Not ₹52,000 salary — but it was mine. No office. No commute. No manager asking for "status updates" every 2 hours.

I also did 3 more barter trades that month: a social media manager got a landing page in exchange for managing my Instagram for a month. A content writer got a WordPress site in exchange for 5 blog posts for my portfolio. A video editor got a portfolio site in exchange for a 60-second intro video.

Zero cash spent. Portfolio growing fast.

The secret? Repeat clients and referrals. That first Chennai business owner referred 4 more people. Each project added a testimonial. Each testimonial made the next sale easier.

The Numbers Don't Lie

Let me compare my two lives:

₹8 LPA Job vs. Freelancing

Job: ₹52,000/month take-home · 10+ hours/day · 2-hour commute · Fixed salary · 15 days leave · Boss decides your growth

Freelancing: ₹60,000-1,00,000/month (6 months in) · 5-6 hours/day · Zero commute · Income scales with skill · Unlimited leave · I decide my growth

But I won't lie — there are trade-offs:

  • No guaranteed salary. Some months are slow. I keep 3 months of expenses as emergency fund.
  • No health insurance. I buy a ₹15,000/year personal plan now.
  • Self-discipline is hard. No manager means nobody pushes you. You have to show up for yourself.
  • Client hunting never stops. Even when you're busy, you need to market yourself for next month's pipeline.

What I'd Tell Anyone Thinking of Quitting

"Don't quit your job on Monday. Start freelancing on Saturday."

Here's my honest advice:

  • Don't quit cold turkey. Start freelancing on weekends while you have a salary. Build 2-3 clients before you hand in your resignation.
  • Use skill barter to build your portfolio. If you have zero clients and zero portfolio, barter on Grit&Gigs. It costs nothing and gives you real work samples.
  • Keep 6 months of expenses saved. Freelancing income is variable. You need a buffer.
  • Pick ONE skill and go deep. Don't be a "do everything" freelancer. Be the best at one thing.
  • Get your first paid client fast. Even ₹500. The psychology of earning from freelancing changes everything.
  • Join a community. Freelancing is lonely. Join freelancer groups, Discord servers, or platforms like Grit&Gigs where you can connect with other freelancers.

Where I Am Now

It's August 2026. Five months since I quit.

Last month I earned ₹85,000 from 4 projects. I'm working on 2 more. I also have 3 active barter partnerships — a designer, a content writer, and a video editor — where we trade skills regularly and save thousands.

I'm not saying freelancing is for everyone. If you love your job, keep it. But if you've ever felt that Sunday evening dread, that "there has to be more" feeling, that frustration of watching your salary barely cover expenses — know that there's another way.

You don't need a fancy degree. You don't need savings. You don't even need money to start.

You just need a skill, a platform, and the guts to try.
